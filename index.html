A blueprint you can interrogate.

Extruding walls from a floor plan is a rendering problem with a fixed answer. Everything after extrusion here is a derivation — a checkable mathematical result, not a rendered guess. Rooms become a graph, doors become edges, and graph theory answers the questions a commander actually asks.

The floor graph is the product

Rooms are nodes, doors and stairs are edges. A bridge door is the only way between two halves of a building; an articulation point room disconnects the graph if you lose it. Those are different questions, so they are drawn in different colours on purpose.

Scale is falsifiable

Door width, wall thickness, corridor width, bounding box and smallest room area are each checked against configurable plausibility bounds. If scale is unknown the state is UNSCALED — areas are refused rather than defaulted, and export and briefing are blocked. A confidently wrong building is the worst thing this system could produce.

Sealed by construction

A runtime kill switch blocks every non-loopback socket, and a build-time linter refuses to compile a network call below the app layer. The geometry runs in TypeScript in the browser: if a perception backend existed and died, the building would still render.

The CV model is not the product. The verified floor graph is the product. Every element carries who created it and whether a human has verified it — and a model that has not been verified cannot reach a commander, because briefing mode is gated on the document status, not on the interface.
